How to choose a Solana wallet cleaner: an 8 point checklist

A wallet cleaner is only useful if you can trust what it asks you to sign. Use these eight checks before you connect.

Updated November 2026

1. It is non-custodial

It never asks for a seed phrase or private key and cannot move funds without your signature.

2. It shows a summary before signing

You should see the SOL released, the fee, and the amount you receive before your wallet opens.

3. The fee is clear and comparable

Fees for rent recovery tools range widely, from about 1% to 20%. Prefer a tool that states its fee in plain text and takes it only from the SOL released, never on top of your balance.

4. It lists what it signs

Look for a page that names every program the transaction touches. You can compare it with your wallet prompt.

5. It simulates first

Simulation catches failing transactions before you sign and prevents broken batches.

6. It handles Token-2022

Newer tokens use extensions that block closing in some cases. A modern tool detects and handles them. See the Token-2022 close error.

7. Burning is opt-in

If a tool can burn tokens, nothing should be selected by default, and valuable tokens should be flagged.

8. You can try it read-only

A free wallet check that needs no connection lets you see the numbers before you decide.
