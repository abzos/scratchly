Questions

Can Scratchly touch my funds?

No. It never has your keys. Every transaction is built in your browser and signed in your own wallet, and you can read what you sign before approving.

What is rent?

Every token account on Solana locks a small deposit of SOL, about 0.00204. When an empty account is closed, the deposit returns to you.

Will it close accounts that hold tokens?

Never by default. Only empty accounts are selected. Tokens with a balance can be burned and closed only if you pick them yourself in Advanced.

How do holder tiers work?

The app reads your token balance when you connect. Holding enough puts you in a lower tier automatically. Nothing is staked or locked.

My wallet shows a warning. What now?

New sites are sometimes flagged until wallets review them. Check that the address is app.scratchly.cfd and that the prompt matches the summary on screen. If anything differs, do not approve.

Where do the live stats come from?

They are counted from Scratchly transactions on Solana and refreshed every few minutes. The fee wallet balance is read live from the network.

Abstract

Scratchly is a non-custodial utility for Solana wallets. It finds empty token accounts, closes them, and returns the SOL deposit locked inside to the wallet owner. A small fee, taken only from the SOL that is released, funds the product. Holding the project token $SCR lowers that fee to 4% or 0%.

This paper describes the problem, the mechanism, the fee model, the trust model, the role of the token, and the roadmap. It is written to be checked: every claim about how the product behaves can be verified on-chain or in the open transaction flow of the app.

The problem

Every token account on Solana holds a rent-exempt deposit of about 0.00203928 SOL. An account is created whenever a wallet receives a new token: a swap, an airdrop, a memecoin purchase. Selling the full balance does not close the account, so the deposit stays locked. Active wallets accumulate dozens or hundreds of these accounts. One hundred empty accounts lock roughly 0.204 SOL.

  • It is invisible. Most wallets do not show empty accounts, so users do not know the SOL is there.
  • It is tedious. Closing accounts one by one inside a wallet takes many taps and confirmations.
  • It is getting more complex. Token-2022 accounts can carry extensions. An account that holds withheld transfer fees cannot be closed until those fees are harvested, and manual attempts fail with obscure error codes.
  • Trust is a barrier. Cleaning tools ask users to sign transactions they cannot easily inspect, and wallets increasingly warn about unfamiliar sites.

The solution

Scratchly reads the connected wallet, lists what can be reclaimed, builds the transactions, and shows one plain summary before anything is signed: SOL returned, fee, and what the user receives. The rent returns to the owner's wallet inside the same transaction that pays the fee. There is no deposit, no account, and no custody.

The product has two modes. Quick clean closes empty accounts and is the default. Advanced lets the user burn tokens they no longer want and close those accounts too. Nothing in Advanced is selected by default, and burning requires an explicit confirmation.

How it works

  1. Connect. The app receives only the public address of the wallet.
  2. Scan. It queries all token accounts owned by the address under both the classic Token program and Token-2022. Accounts are sorted into empty, holding a balance, or frozen. Frozen accounts are never touched. Token-2022 accounts with withheld transfer fees are flagged.
  3. Plan. Closures are grouped into transactions of up to 18 closes (up to 7 when burning). The fee is calculated per transaction from the SOL being released.
  4. Simulate. Every transaction is simulated against the network before the wallet is asked to sign. If one account cannot be closed, it is dropped automatically and the rest continue. The result sets an explicit compute budget.
  5. Sign. The only signer is the user's wallet. Each transaction is a separate prompt, so every approval matches what the user sees on screen.
  6. Send and confirm. The signed transaction goes to the network with preflight checks on. The app shows the transaction links.
  7. Record. Public statistics are derived from the on-chain transactions that involve the fee wallet.

What a transaction contains

  • CloseAccount with the owner as the destination of the released SOL.
  • Optionally Burn, only for tokens the user selected in Advanced.
  • Where needed, HarvestWithheldTokensToMint so that a Token-2022 account becomes closable. Anyone can run this instruction.
  • A SystemProgram transfer of the displayed fee to the fee wallet.

Security and trust model

Scratchly is non-custodial. It has no access to private keys or seed phrases and cannot move funds without a signature from the owner. The instructions above are the complete list of what the app can ask a wallet to do.

What users should check

  • Open the app only from app.scratchly.cfd.
  • Compare the wallet prompt with the on-screen summary before approving.
  • Use a wallet with transaction simulation turned on.

Known limits

  • There is no smart contract. The app composes standard instructions of existing programs. This keeps the attack surface small, and it also means the fee is applied by the front end rather than enforced by a contract.
  • Front-end integrity matters. The site is static and its source is versioned. Users who want more certainty can verify the instructions in their wallet prompt.
  • Third-party services supply the RPC connection, the Solana client library, and market data. An outage degrades the experience but cannot move funds.
  • Wallets may warn about new domains or about transactions that close accounts. We work with wallet teams on review and keep the transaction format as simple and transparent as possible.

Fee model

The fee is a percentage of the SOL released by closing accounts. It is never charged on top of the user's balance, and it is shown before signing. Network fees (about 0.000005 SOL per signature plus a small compute cost) are paid by the user as on any Solana transaction.

Example

A wallet with 100 empty token accounts releases 0.203928 SOL. At the Standard tier the fee is about 0.0184 SOL and the user receives about 0.1856 SOL. At the Holder tier the fee is about 0.0082 SOL and the user receives about 0.1957 SOL. At the Diamond tier the user receives the full 0.2039 SOL.

Minimum balance rule. A new Solana account must hold at least about 0.00089 SOL. While the fee wallet is below that minimum, a fee smaller than the gap would make the transaction fail, so the app skips the fee for that transaction instead. The user keeps the full amount.

Market context

Publicly listed fees among comparable rent-recovery tools range from roughly 1% to 20% as of November 2026. The Standard tier sits in the middle of that range. The tiers give holders a path to a lower or zero fee, and live fee reporting lets anyone see where the money goes.

The token

$SCR is a utility token launched on pump.fun. Its role inside the product is simple: your balance at the moment you clean decides your fee tier.

  • The app reads the balance of the connected wallet when it scans. No staking, lock-up, or transfer is needed.
  • Thresholds are 1M and 5M tokens. With the standard pump.fun supply of 1,000,000,000 these are 0.1% and 0.5% of supply.
  • Tiers are applied to the Solana wallet that signs the transaction.
What the token is not. It gives no ownership of the project, no claim on revenue, no dividends, and no governance rights. Its price can fall to zero. Nothing in this paper promises a return.

Fee revenue is received by the public fee wallet. It supports operations, development, and growth. The team is working on a published fee-use policy, which may include buybacks. No amounts or timing are promised.

Transparency and metrics

The fee wallet address is public on the site, and its balance is read live from the network. The counters on the site (SOL reclaimed, accounts closed, wallets cleaned, fees earned) are calculated by an automated job from public transactions that involve the fee wallet. Refresh takes a few minutes, so the numbers can lag. A clean you complete yourself is added to your screen immediately.

Statistics are informational. Because anyone can create a transaction that touches the fee wallet, they should be read as an indicator, not as an audited figure. Token price and market data come from public DEX data.

Risks

  • Market risk. The token is volatile and can lose all of its value.
  • Technical risk. Software can contain bugs. RPC providers, wallets, or the network can have outages or change behaviour.
  • Wallet warnings. New sites may be flagged by wallets until reviewed.
  • Irreversibility. Burning tokens cannot be undone. A closed account can be created again, and its deposit is paid again when a token is received.
  • Regulatory risk. Rules for crypto assets differ by country. Users are responsible for their own compliance and taxes.
  • Dependency risk. The product relies on third-party infrastructure.
